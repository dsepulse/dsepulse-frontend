The six signal engines

Each runs independently after the close, in sequence, and writes its own result. None overwrites another, and they are not merged into a single number.

  • Daily Picker — a momentum scan of every listed stock, reading price action, volume surge against the stock's own average, exchange category, price-to-earnings and the bid-ask spread. The broadest net, and the one that publishes entry, target and stop.
  • WMA Analysis — weighted moving averages over 9, 34 and 89 sessions from 150 days of history, with an alert when a crossover is fresh rather than weeks old.
  • Tech Analysis — a score out of 100 combining the moving-average stack, MACD 12/26/9, RSI 14, volume surge and Bollinger Bands.
  • BD Signal — a score out of 100 tuned to this market: DSEX mood, a stop derived from Average True Range, a sector adjustment, consecutive up days, and a circuit check. It also rejects illiquid names outright rather than scoring them.
  • Combined Signal — triple confirmation. A stock appears only where the Picker, WMA and Tech engines each clear their own threshold on the same name on the same day. The shortest list.
  • Smart Money — stocks trading on abnormal volume, and whether the large blocks are buying or selling. No entry or stop, by design.

The three tools that are not engines

Screener

The engines apply fixed conditions. The screener lets you apply your own across the whole listed market.

Risk calculator

The calculator turns a stop distance and a maximum acceptable loss into a position size. It is the least glamorous tool here and the one that most affects outcomes.

Track record

The track record reports what previously published signals went on to do. It is a record, not a forecast, and it carries no implication about any future signal.

How the tools relate

A reasonable sequence is: read the Daily Picker or Combined Signal for candidates, check the WMA trend for context, size the position with the calculator, and use the track record to set expectations about how often this kind of setup has previously worked.

What the tools do not do is make the decision. Every one of them reports conditions that were true at the last close. Acting on them is a separate judgement, and it stays with you.
