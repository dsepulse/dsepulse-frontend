Questions about DSE market analysis

Twelve questions that come up most often, answered directly. Where the answer is “no” or “it cannot”, it says so.

What is DSE Pulse?

A market-analysis service for the Dhaka Stock Exchange. After each trading session it applies six independent sets of technical conditions to every listed stock and publishes the names that met them, together with the price levels those conditions imply.

How often is it updated?

Once per trading day, after the close. The engines run in sequence between roughly 4:35 PM and 5:30 PM Bangladesh time. Nothing updates during the trading session.

Is DSE Pulse investment advice?

No. It publishes technical analysis of publicly listed securities. It is not advice, not a recommendation to buy or sell, and not a personalised assessment of anyone's circumstances.

Does it predict share prices?

No. It filters a universe of over 400 listed stocks to those that met defined conditions at the last close, and states the levels that follow from those conditions. It makes no forecast and promises no return.

How are entry, target and stop decided?

Mechanically, not by judgement. The Daily Picker anchors a buy zone to the session's low and places a fixed ladder of targets above it with a stop below. BD Signal derives its stop from Average True Range instead, so a more volatile stock gets a wider stop.

Do all the signals come with levels?

No. Tech Analysis, WMA Analysis and Smart Money publish no entry, target or stop of their own. Where levels appear beside those signals they are borrowed from an engine that computes them, and the source is named. Smart Money borrows none at all.

Which moving averages does it use?

Weighted moving averages over 9, 34 and 89 sessions, computed from 150 days of price history. The signal comes from the order of the three averages rather than their values.

Why is there no put-call ratio or implied volatility?

The Dhaka Stock Exchange has no options market, so indicators derived from options pricing have no inputs. Publishing a proxy under the familiar name would be misleading.

What happens if the daily run fails?

Setups stop appearing. Every published setup is bound to the most recent completed run, so if the engines do not run, stocks stop showing a current setup together rather than continuing to display levels from an earlier date.

Are illiquid stocks included?

No. Names that do not trade enough for the volume measures to mean anything are rejected rather than scored. A thinly traded stock can post a large percentage move on very little money, which would otherwise dominate any ranking.

What does it cost to trade on the figures shown?

Exchange and regulatory charges come to roughly 0.025% of turnover for a round trip. Brokerage commission is on top and varies by broker, so it is not assumed anywhere in the system. Every published level is gross of commission.
