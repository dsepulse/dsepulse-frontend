Screening the Dhaka Stock Exchange

A screener's job is subtraction. On a market of 400+ listed names, the value is not in scoring everything — it is in removing everything that cannot be acted on, and being honest about why.

What a screen is actually for

A stock screener applies a set of conditions to every name in a market and returns the ones that match. The conditions are the whole product. Choose them badly and the output is a long list of stocks that look interesting and cannot be traded.

On the Dhaka Stock Exchange the conditions worth screening on fall into three groups: what the price did, whether anyone was trading it, and where it sits in its own range.

The liquidity trap

This is the failure mode that catches most screens on this market, and it is worth being concrete about.

A stock that trades a handful of times in a session can post a large percentage move on almost no money. It will top a screen sorted by "biggest gainers" and be the worst name on the list — the move is an artefact of thin trading, and attempting to buy it moves the price against you immediately.

The answer is not to rank such stocks lower. It is to reject them outright, before scoring, so they cannot appear at all. DSE Pulse's BD Signal engine does exactly that: names whose volume is too low for the volume measures to mean anything are rejected rather than scored, and the rejection is recorded.

Criteria that carry information here

CriterionWhy it matters on this market
Volume vs its own average Absolute volume says more about a company's size than about today. A stock trading three times its own normal volume is the signal; the raw number is not.
Move against the index A 2% gain on a day the DSEX fell is a different event from the same gain on a day it rose. Screens that ignore market direction treat them identically.
Position in the 52-week range Context for the current price. DSE Pulse computes this range from closing prices rather than intraday extremes, so a single erratic tick cannot define a stock's "high".
Exchange category The exchange assigns listed companies to categories. DSE Pulse's Daily Picker reads that category as one of its inputs.
Moving-average alignment Whether short, medium and long averages are stacked in the same direction — a trend filter rather than a trigger.

Each of those is a filter, not a forecast. Together they reduce 400+ names to something a person can read.

What a screen cannot tell you

A screen reports that conditions were met at the last close. It does not know why, it does not know what is coming, and it does not know your position size or your tolerance for being wrong.

Costs are not in the screen

Round-trip exchange and regulatory charges on the DSE come to roughly 0.025% of turnover, with brokerage commission on top and varying by broker. A screen that surfaces a 1.5% opportunity has not accounted for what it costs to take it — particularly on a small position.
